Referee Comments:
Referee #1 (Comments to Both Author and Editor):

The revision has responded to the editor's concern by foregrounding the fluid physics, and manuscript's investigations are worth publishing. However, in its present form the contribution is obscured by excessive length and writing that is very hard to follow. I recommend major revision.

Major Comments:

1. In my opinion the manuscript length needs to be reduced substantially. The core content could be presented in 15-20 pages. Specifically, standard LBM methodology (Secs. 3.2-3.3), the database protocol (Sec. 3.4), and much of the observable-by-observable reporting in Section 4 can be moved to the supplementary material.

2. The abstract needs to be rewritten, it is hard to follow the methodology and motivation of the study in the abstract. It needs a clear narrative style that states the question, the finding, and why it matters. The same density problem affects the whole manuscript (especially Section 5).

3. The D/A minimum is a 0.4-0.9% trough, while the reported resolution bias between N=128 and N=256 is 19.5-25.6%. The authors argue the geometry-relative ordering is stable to ~2%, but a sub-percent feature resting on a 20%+ biased baseline needs more than spot checks at selected Re. In my opinion, it is required to either extend the N=256 (or finer) campaign across the parameter space, or state the limitation far more prominently and temper the conclusions accordingly.

4. It is suggested to remove or substantially shorten the DeepONet section (Sec. 6). It still runs ~6 pages, does not inform the physical conclusions, and its 30-50% dissipation errors make it unusable as a quantitative check. The accuracy-consistency decoupling finding is interesting but belongs in a separate, ML-focused paper. Removing it would sharpen the physical narrative considerably.

Minor Comments:

1. Table III~V are excessive, the titles are too long. If possible, these should be included in supplementary material or use a figure or summary where relevant.

Recommendation:

There manuscript presents interesting investigation on the D vs. D/A distinction and the wall-band decomposition in a continuously deformed cavity family. The work needs to be cut to a focused paper, rewritten for clarity, and better supported on resolution. These changes are suggested to offer clarity to PoF readers by presenting focused and core findings.


Referee #2 (Comments to Both Author and Editor):

This manuscript investigates the effects of continuous geometric deformation on the flow structures and viscous dissipation in superelliptic lid-driven cavities. The authors employ a multiple-relaxation-time lattice Boltzmann method, cross-validated with a finite-element solver, and further examine geometry extrapolation using a physics-informed DeepONet.

The topic is interesting and relevant to computational fluid dynamics. I believe the manuscript can be considered for publication if the following issues are carefully addressed.

Major remarks:
1. In Fig. 2, the x-axis tick labels overlap with the x-axis and should be adjusted. The axis labels in Figs. 4, 5, 8, 9, and 10, as well as the legends in Figs. 6 and 7, are too small. Please increase the font sizes to improve readability.
2. The authors acknowledge that the adissipation changes by approximately 19.5-25.6% between the 128 x 128 and 256 x 256 grids. This is a substantial numerical discrepancy, particularly because viscous dissipation is the central quantity investigated in this study. A more convincing grid-convergence study, preferably including higher resolutions, is needed.
3. The manuscript reports cross-validation against a Taylor-Hood finite-element formulation. However, the principal conclusions depend on velocity gradients and viscous dissipation rather than velocity fields alone. The authors should demonstrate quantitative agreement between LBM and FEM for integrated dissipation.
4. The moving and stationary boundaries meet at locations where the prescribed wall velocities are discontinuous. Such junctions can produce strong localized velocity gradients and affect the convergence of viscous dissipation. The authors should clarify how these junctions are treated numerically and quantify their contribution to the integrated dissipation across different geometries.
5. A schematic illustrating the overall DeepONet implementation framework is not provided. The authors should include a diagram showing the network architecture, inputs and outputs, training procedure, and incorporation of physics-informed constraints to improve the clarity of the methodology.

Referee #6 (Comments to Both Author and Editor):

This paper studies geometry and Reynolds-number effects in deformable lid-driven cavities. It includes many results, but I found the manuscript very difficult to follow. The main contribution and its relevance to fluid mechanics are still unclear to me. The paper is also far too long: there are so many figures, tables, and sensitivity analyses that it is easy to lose sight of the main findings. The ML part feels only loosely connected to the flow study.

## Abstract

The abstract reports many results, but the main message gets lost among the details. Please rewrite it to explain the purpose of the work, its main findings, and why they matter. The ML part also arrives abruptly.

## Introduction and background

- The motivation and contribution need to be stated more clearly and concisely.
- The superelliptic exponent is defined, but its physical meaning and effect on the cavity shape should be explained for non-expert readers.
- Please also make the reason for including the ML study clearer.

## Section 3.3

The solver verification is useful, but the section could be shortened. Move detailed code comparisons and supporting verification results to the Supplementary Material.

## Sections 4 and 5

- These sections are much too long and difficult to follow. They contain so many results and sensitivity analyses that I was left unsure what the main conclusions are. Please select the most relevant results, organize the discussion around a few clear findings, and move supporting analyses to the Supplementary Material.

- The dissipation results also show substantial resolution sensitivity, while the reported minimum is shallow. Please clarify which conclusions are robust and how much confidence readers should place in the proposed physical explanation.

## Section 6

The ML study is interesting, but its relevance to the main fluid-mechanics contribution is unclear. What does the neural operator demonstrate that the solver itself cannot? Please explain why this method and test are useful for this problem, or reduce the emphasis on this part.

## Section 7 and 8

The take-home messages are difficult to retain after the long results sections. Please make the discussion and conclusions shorter, and distinguish the robust findings from resolution-sensitive results and possible explanations.

Reviewer 4: Review Report 
Geometry-Induced Reorganization of Viscous Dissipation in Continuously 
Deformable Superelliptic Lid-Driven Cavities 
By BENIAICHE Ahmed 
General Comments: 
This study investigates steady two-dimensional flow in a family of superelliptic lid-driven 
cavities across ten geometry exponents and nineteen Reynolds numbers up to Re = 1000. 
Reference solutions are obtained using a D2Q9 multiple-relaxation-time lattice Boltzmann 
method, with selected velocity fields compared against an independent finite-element 
formulation. The analysis examines kinetic energy, primary recirculation, vorticity, and viscous 
dissipation, including the distinction between total and area-normalized dissipation and the 
contributions of the wall layer and core. A physics-informed DeepONet is also evaluated outside 
the training geometry range. The topic is relevant to Physics of Fluids, and the continuous 
geometry family offers a useful framework for examining the combined influence of boundary 
geometry and wall motion on viscous losses. 
The manuscript acknowledges several important limitations, including the resolution dependence 
of absolute dissipation, the shallow dissipation minima, the threshold dependence of secondary
eddy detection, and the distinction between differential residuals and integrated-observable 
accuracy. However, some interpretations and robustness claims elsewhere remain inconsistent with 
these qualifications. The stated square-cavity limit requires correction, the curvature measures 
need verification against the circular case, and the dissipation and power-balance calculations 
require closer examination of the boundary-junction treatment. The simulation inventory and 
fitting procedures also need clarification. I recommend major revision to address these issues and 
ensure that the physical conclusions are supported by the numerical evidence. 
Comments: 
INTRODUCTION 
1. Section 2 already places the study in the context of shape-dependent cavity flows and 
dissipation-based shape optimization. The Introduction would benefit from a more 
precise statement of the principal new physical finding. The difference between the 
minima of D and D/A follows partly from the changing area and does not, by itself, 
establish a new flow mechanism. Please distinguish the new observations from the 
proposed explanations and identify the quantitative evidence supporting the principal 
physical contribution. 
2. Sections 3.1 and 7.2 acknowledge that geometry and boundary conditions vary together. 
However, the statements in Sections 2.1 and 3.2 suggesting that area normalization 
separates domain-size effects from geometric redistribution require qualification. Varying 
n changes the moving-arc length and the direction of the imposed velocity, as well as the 
cavity area and curvature. Please identify which conclusions describe this coupled 
configuration. Reporting moving-wall length and consistently evaluated injected power 
would help characterize the forcing. A controlled comparison would be needed to support 
an isolated geometric attribution; alternatively, the conclusions can remain explicitly 
limited to the coupled geometry-and-forcing configuration. 
NUMERICAL METHODS & CASE DESCRIPTION 
1. The square-cavity limit stated in Section 3.1 requires correction. With the moving 
boundary defined by y > 0 and the velocity prescribed as U_lid(m_y, −m_x), the limit n 
→ ∞ includes motion along the upper halves of both vertical side walls as well as the top 
wall. For example, the upper half of the right side wall has limiting velocity (0, −U_lid), 
while its lower half remains stationary. This differs from the classical cavity with 
stationary side walls. Please clarify the implemented boundary condition and distinguish 
verification against a separately configured classical square-cavity benchmark from 
verification of the limiting member of the present geometry family. 
2. The moving and stationary boundary conditions meet discontinuously at (±1, 0), as 
acknowledged in Section S13. Such junctions can generate singular velocity gradients, so 
a finite, grid-independent full-domain dissipation cannot be assumed without examining 
the boundary treatment. Section 5.2 also explains that the production gradient mask 
excludes the wall-adjacent layer, introducing a cutoff that changes with resolution. Please 
specify any regularization used in the reference simulations, its physical length scale, and 
whether that scale remains fixed under refinement. If the boundary velocity is 
unregularized, assess the singular contribution and cutoff dependence, and distinguish the 
reported masked dissipation from any claimed continuum full-domain quantity. 
3. The window-averaged discrete curvature calculation in Section S15, Table S15, and 
Figure 11 requires verification against the circular geometry. For n = 2 and a = b = 1, the 
boundary is a unit circle with curvature equal to one everywhere, and averaging over an 
arclength window should preserve that value. However, Table S15 reports a maximum 
curvature of 29.87, an RMS curvature of 4.015, and a sharp-region dissipation fraction of 
0.087 at Re = 100. These values deviate substantially from the analytical circular result, 
for which the stated sharp-set criterion should select no boundary points. Although 
Section S15 explicitly defines the measure as discrete, the magnitude of these deviations 
requires explanation. Please identify their source, verify the procedure against the 
analytical circular result under refinement while retaining the prescribed physical 
averaging window, and reassess the associated correlations and interpretations using the 
verified curvature measures. 
4. The power-balance verification in Section S19 and its interpretation in Section 5.2 
require a complete control-volume formulation. Removing the junction disks creates 
artificial interfaces across which kinetic energy, pressure work, and viscous work can be 
transported. Agreement between the remaining lid power and dissipation outside the 
disks therefore does not establish a closed energy balance unless these interface terms are 
included or shown to be negligible. Please report the balance on a consistently defined 
domain and explain the whole-domain discrepancies of approximately 20.8% and 31.3% 
between the momentum-exchange power and the layer-restored dissipation in Table S22. 
The implications for verification of the production estimator should be stated explicitly. 
A check on representative superelliptic production geometries, or another quantitatively 
justified verification, would establish the relevance of this square-cavity diagnostic to the 
main results. 
5. The independent FEM comparison is useful, but Table S7 assesses interior velocity 
discrepancies at Re = 100 and 400 while excluding boundary-adjacent nodes. This does 
not directly establish the accuracy of wall shear or dissipation at Re = 1000. Please 
provide quantitative verification targeted to the gradient-dependent quantities supporting 
the main conclusions. Comparisons of velocity gradients, wall traction, or integrated 
dissipation are possible approaches; equivalent diagnostics would also be suitable. 
Section 4.2 correctly notes that U_max includes prescribed moving-wall values and may 
saturate at U_lid. The velocity-convergence claim in Section 5.2 should therefore be 
supported by field norms or interior profiles. 
6. Please reconcile the simulation inventory with the data used to fit the extrema. Table II 
and Table S21 describe a 30-case N = 256 campaign at ten exponents, whereas Figure 6 
and Table V appear to use additional closely spaced geometries. Provide the exact 
exponents, raw observable values, and fitting windows at both resolutions, identifying 
any additional runs or interpolation. In particular, the Table V caption attributes a missing 
point in the N = 128, Re = 1000 density fit to n = 2.25, although the reported minimum is 
near n = 4.05. That point cannot lie within the stated local ±0.5 window. Please correct 
the caption or explain the actual fitting support. An accessible repository or reviewer
access link should be supplied, or the underlying fit data and scripts should accompany 
the revision. 
7. The pressure treatment and the distinction between B1-PI and B1-pinit require 
clarification. Section S13 specifies a pressure weight of 0.25 and a pressure normalization 
constant, but the displayed objective contains no explicit loss term corresponding to that 
weight. It also describes changes to the pressure-head bias and a scalar addend. An 
additive pressure constant alone leaves ∇p, and hence the stated momentum residual, 
unchanged. Please provide the complete objective, any pressure supervision or gauge 
constraint, and the precise initialization changes. If the variants differ only by additive 
pressure offsets, explain which other loss or implementation feature allows those offsets 
to affect training; if additional parameters differ, identify them. 
8. Please reconcile the descriptions of the training and reported residuals. Section S13 
specifies automatic differentiation, whereas Section S4 states that the neural and LBM 
residuals use identical finite-difference operators and sampling, and Table S4 refers to the 
finite-difference stencil as the training residual operator. Section 7.1 describes the 
reported metrics as normalized by their initial magnitudes, while Section S13 defines 
them as spatial mean squares. Different operators and normalizations can be appropriate 
at different stages, but their uses need to be explicit. For each reported metric, specify the 
derivative operator, sampling, integration mask, normalization, and treatment of the 
junction neighborhoods. Confirm whether the reported neural and LBM divergence 
residuals already use a common finite-difference evaluation and explain its relationship 
to the automatic-differentiation formulation. If they do not, provide a common evaluation 
to support the comparison. An independently sampled, denser evaluation would also help 
assess sensitivity to the 512-point sampling. 
RESULTS AND DISCUSSION 
1. Section 4.4 appropriately describes the dissipation trough as a shallow plateau and 
qualifies the sampled crossings as resolution- and normalization-dependent. Table V also 
provides useful evidence of location stability between two resolutions. However, Section 
5.2 states that every relative statement in n is unaffected by resolution, while Sections 
5.3 and 7.1 describe the geometry-relative response as resolution-independent to about 2%. 
These broader claims require further qualification. The reported 19.5–25.6% changes in 
the representative refinement study are measured differences between N = 128 and 256, 
not established errors relative to a converged solution. A nearly common multiplicative 
refinement factor can preserve relative ordering despite a substantial absolute change. 
The relevant question is whether the geometry-dependent numerical uncertainty near the 
trough is sufficiently small to resolve the 0.03–0.36% margins in Table IV. Please assess 
uncertainty in local differences between geometries and fitted minimum locations, 
including sensitivity to junction treatment and fitting windows. If that precision cannot be 
established, retain the broad low-dissipation-range interpretation consistently and present 
the crossing Reynolds numbers as sampling-dependent diagnostics. 
2. Section 4.4 explicitly distinguishes the gradient-valid mask area A_mask from the exact 
cavity area A(n), but this distinction is not maintained consistently in Sections 3.2 and 
4.3. Please use unambiguous notation for D/A_mask and KE/A(n) throughout and assess 
whether the main geometry comparisons depend materially on the mask and excluded 
wall region. Section 5.3 also states that a larger domain can have the same or smaller total 
loss while having a larger loss per unit area. For positive areas and losses, this statement 
is algebraically incorrect and should be corrected. Section 2 already notes that area 
normalization is not equivalent to volume-constrained optimization; the fixed-volume 
interpretation in Section 5.3 should be made consistent with that qualification. 
3. Section 5.2 correctly recognizes that the wall-band diagnostic describes spatial 
distribution without establishing causation. However, the stronger interpretations in 
Sections 4.4 and 5.3 require an additive quantitative decomposition. Figure 6 compares 
D_core/A_core with D/A_mask, which have different denominators and therefore do not 
directly show how the wall and core contributions add to the reported loss density. Please 
provide contributions such as D_wall/A_mask and D_core/A_mask, with the wall and 
core forming a partition of the same integration mask. A decreasing wall-band fraction 
alone does not exclude an increase in the absolute wall contribution. These results should 
support the proposed mechanism, or the interpretation should be qualified accordingly. 
Spatial dissipation distributions and sensitivity to wall-band thickness would provide 
additional support. 
4. Section 4.6 appropriately acknowledges that the the secondary extremum persists under 
refinement while falling below the 1% threshold on the finer grids. Nevertheless, the 
captions of Figures 9 and 10 use onset language, and Section 7.1 describes the near-
square case as topologically distinct. The interval 950 < Re ≤ 1000 brackets a detection
threshold crossing at N = 128 and does not establish the onset of a new flow topology. 
Please carry this distinction consistently into those passages, using the reported 
continuous extremum amplitudes and streamline evidence. Also reconcile the search
region definition: Table S19 reports corner distances of approximately 0.272 L despite 
specifying a search within 0.25 L of a corner. The stated lack of a demonstrated causal 
link between this weak eddy and the dissipation increase should remain explicit 
throughout. 
5. The abstract reports substantial dissipation errors, Section 6.3 refers to Table S20, and 
Section 7.2 acknowledges that lower differential residuals do not guarantee better 
integrated observables. However, Section 7.1 also treats the operator results as evidence 
that the physical structure of the reference solutions is preserved. The changes between 
variants should therefore be discussed quantitatively in Section 6.3. For the seed
averaged fields in Table S20, extending the physics pool from B1-pinit to B1-train98 
increases mean dissipation error from 29.2% to 36.4%, kinetic-energy error from 2.8% to 
4.5%, and primary-streamfunction error from 1.2% to 2.4%, despite reducing the 
divergence residual. Please distinguish the demonstrated residual improvements from the 
accuracy of these observables. If individual-model performance is being assessed,
compute observables for each seed before summarizing their errors. If the ensemble
mean field is the intended predictor, report its performance separately, since evaluating 
quadratic dissipation after averaging fields measures a different quantity. 
CONCLUSION 
1. After addressing the verification issues, revise the Abstract and Conclusion so that broad 
trends, fitted minimum locations, proposed mechanisms, and operator results retain their 
respective levels of support. In particular, distinguish the observed low-dissipation range 
from precisely resolved optimum locations, and the proposed interior contribution from 
an established explanation of the near-square dissipation increase. The reduced neural 
residuals should also be distinguished from accurate recovery of integrated observables. 
The existing two-dimensional and parameter-range limitations are appropriate; the 
qualifications concerning boundary treatment, curvature evaluation, and dissipation 
accuracy should be reflected consistently in the final claims. 
MINOR COMMENTS 
1. Section 4.3 describes kinetic energy as total momentum content; these are different 
physical quantities, and the wording should be corrected. Section 5.1 states that viscosity 
does not shift the preferred geometry despite the Reynolds-dependent drift discussed 
elsewhere. Please distinguish persistence of a broad low-loss band from movement of its 
fitted centre. 
2. The field labels and color scales in Figures 3 and 9, and the overlaid scales in Figure 11, 
are difficult to read at normal page size. Please enlarge the relevant panels and identify 
each plotted quantity clearly. Use comparable color scales when comparing the same 
quantity across geometries, or explain any necessary differences. 
Reviewer 5: The revised manuscript investigates the effects of continuous geometric 
deformation on the flow structure and viscous dissipation in superelliptic lid
driven cavities using the lattice Boltzmann method, supplemented by a physics
informed neural operator. The authors have made considerable efforts to 
reorganize the manuscript and emphasize the underlying fluid physics in 
response to the editor's comments. The systematic parametric investigation and 
the distinction between total and area-normalized viscous dissipation are 
appreciated. 
However, I still have substantial concerns regarding the scientific novelty, the 
robustness of the numerical results, and the depth of the physical interpretation. 
In my opinion, the present study does not yet provide sufficiently convincing 
evidence of new fluid-physics insights to justify publication in Physics of Fluids. 
My major concerns are summarized below. 
1. Limited scientific novelty and unclear advancement beyond existing 
studies. 
The effects of cavity geometry on vortex structures, flow redistribution, and 
viscous dissipation have been extensively investigated in previous studies. 
Although the present work considers a continuously deformable superelliptic 
geometry, most of the reported findings appear to be extensions of established 
physical understanding rather than fundamentally new insights. The authors 
should clarify what new physical principles or mechanisms have been identified 
beyond the existing literature on geometry-dependent cavity flows and dissipation 
minimization. 
2. Insufficient numerical convergence of the key physical quantities. 
The authors acknowledge that the calculated viscous dissipation changes by 
approximately 19.5–25.6% when the grid resolution increases from 128 × 128 to 
256 × 256. This is a considerable discrepancy, particularly because viscous 
dissipation and its geometric dependence constitute the central findings of the 
manuscript. Although the relative ordering of the geometries appears reasonably 
stable, this does not fully establish the convergence of the reported dissipation 
minima or their Reynolds-number dependence. A more rigorous convergence 
assessment of the near-wall velocity gradients and integrated dissipation is 
necessary to support these conclusions. 
3. Insufficient quantitative evidence supporting the proposed physical 
mechanisms. 
The authors attribute the non-monotonic dissipation behavior to the competition 
between bulk circulation and localized near-wall shear. However, the proposed 
mechanism is primarily supported by post-processing of the velocity fields, 
curvature-related diagnostics, and fixed-thickness wall-layer decomposition. 
These observations are useful but do not establish a direct causal relationship 
between geometric deformation and the redistribution of viscous dissipation. 
Moreover, the near-wall dissipation remains sensitive to grid resolution. 
Additional quantitative analysis is needed to demonstrate how the individual 
physical contributions govern the observed dissipation minimum. 
4. Limited contribution and predictive capability of the physics-informed 
neural operator. 
The neural operator achieves relatively high velocity-field reconstruction 
accuracy, but its performance is close to that of the data-only baseline in the out
of-distribution cases. More importantly, the errors in integrated viscous 
dissipation remain approximately 30–50%, despite the reduction in the 
divergence residual. This raises concerns regarding the ability of the proposed 
approach to accurately predict the physical quantities central to this study. The 
current results demonstrate an improvement in selected numerical residuals but 
do not convincingly establish improved prediction of the underlying fluid physics. 
5. Limited generality and physical significance of the conclusions. 
The study is restricted to steady two-dimensional flows, a fixed aspect ratio, and 
a single geometry parameter. Furthermore, the geometry deformation 
simultaneously changes the cavity domain and the imposed moving-wall velocity 
distribution. Consequently, the individual effects of geometric confinement, 
boundary curvature, and wall kinematics cannot be independently identified. This 
limits the generality of the proposed physical interpretation. The authors should 
distinguish more clearly between observations specific to the selected geometry 
family and physical mechanisms that may be applicable to more general cavity 
flows. 
Overall assessment 
While the revised manuscript is better organized and contains an extensive 
numerical investigation, the principal contributions remain largely descriptive, and 
the central physical interpretations are not sufficiently supported by converged 
numerical evidence and quantitative mechanistic analysis. Addressing these 
concerns would require substantial additional investigation rather than further 
clarification or restructuring of the manuscript. Therefore, I cannot recommend 
the manuscript for publication in its present form.
